### What it does in plain English
Nyxuri helps you transform a standard Arch Linux computer into a sleek, modern workspace with a single command. It acts like a smart organizer that installs beautiful visual themes and handy tools while keeping your personal files safe. You can easily switch between different looks or turn off specific features without losing your custom changes.

### Who it's for
* **Arch Linux users** who want a polished, professional-looking desktop without spending hours manually editing configuration files.
* **Visual tinkerers** who enjoy changing wallpapers, colors, and window styles but want a system that remembers their preferences.
* **Newcomers to Linux** who want a "ready-to-use" experience that still allows for deep customization later on.

### Key features
* **Modular Design:** You can turn specific tools on or off (like terminal themes or login screens) without affecting the rest of your system.
* **Safe Updates:** The system automatically protects your personal edits, ensuring your custom settings are never overwritten during updates.
* **Dynamic Colors:** It can generate a matching color scheme for your entire desktop based on your chosen wallpaper, creating a cohesive look.
* **Easy Management:** A simple command-line tool lets you install, update, or check for issues with your setup in seconds.
* **Multiple Window Managers:** Supports different window management styles, allowing you to switch how your windows behave without relearning everything.

### How to install (Download)
Nyxuri is installed directly on your computer using a simple command in your terminal. There is no traditional installer file to download.

* **Official Website:** [Visit the Nyxuri Website](https://nyxniri.com)
* **Installation Guide:** [Read the Wiki for Setup Instructions](https://github.com/ech678/Nyxuri/wiki/Home)

### Pros and Cons
* **Pros:** Completely free, keeps your personal settings safe during updates, and offers a very clean, modern aesthetic out of the box.
* **Cons:** Requires basic comfort with using a terminal (command line) to install and manage, and is only compatible with Arch Linux and similar systems.
