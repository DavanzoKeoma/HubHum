### What it does in plain English
Howdy brings convenient facial recognition login capabilities to your Linux computer. It uses your built-in camera and infrared sensors to verify your identity whenever your system asks for confirmation. Working directly within the standard login and terminal systems, it eliminates the need to constantly type your password for everyday administrative tasks.

### Who it's for
* Linux users who want a quicker, more convenient way to log into their desktop environment.
* System administrators looking for a modern authentication workflow for their everyday workstations.
* Privacy-conscious individuals who prefer running local verification tools entirely on their own hardware.

### Key features
* **Seamless integration:** Works everywhere your system normally requires a password, including logins and lock screens.
* **Infrared camera support:** Utilizes built-in infrared hardware for reliable recognition even in low-light conditions.
* **Customizable settings:** Easily manage face models, adjust recognition sensitivity, or disable the tool through simple commands.
* **Command-line management:** Quickly add, remove, or test face profiles using straightforward terminal commands.
* **Graphical companion:** Includes an optional user-friendly interface package for easier setup and configuration.

### Pros and Cons
* **Pros:** Free and open-source, dramatically speeds up daily login routines, integrates natively with existing Linux authentication systems.
* **Cons:** Less secure than a traditional strong password, requires a compatible camera sensor and manual terminal configuration on some distributions.
