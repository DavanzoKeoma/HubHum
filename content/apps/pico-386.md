### What it does in plain English
This project allows you to transform a small Raspberry Pi Pico 2 board into a functional vintage computer. It mimics the hardware of a 1990s-era PC, enabling you to play classic games and run old software directly on the hardware. It handles everything from screen graphics and sound effects to virtual disk drives, providing a complete retro experience.

### Who it's for
* Retro-gaming enthusiasts who want to play classic DOS titles on modern, low-power hardware.
* Hobbyists and makers interested in exploring how vintage computer systems work at the hardware level.

### Key features
* **Authentic Retro Experience:** Emulates a 386SX processor, providing the speed and compatibility needed for many classic DOS programs.
* **Versatile Display Support:** Automatically detects and outputs to either HDMI or VGA screens.
* **Classic Sound Emulation:** Supports a wide range of vintage audio standards, including Sound Blaster, AdLib, and MIDI.
* **Flexible Storage:** Uses SD cards or internal flash memory to load your own disk images, allowing you to manage your software collection easily.
* **Input Support:** Connects standard PC keyboards, mice, and joysticks to complete the authentic feel.

### How to install (Download)
To get started, you will need a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 (or the recommended Frank M2 board).
1. [Download the latest firmware (.uf2)](https://github.com/xrip/pico-386/releases/download/v4.0.0/m2p2-386-C500-P125MHz.uf2).
2. Hold the **BOOTSEL** button on your board while connecting it to your computer via USB.
3. Drag and drop the downloaded file onto the board's storage drive. The system will restart and boot automatically once the transfer is finished.

### Pros and Cons
* **Pros:** Completely free and open-source, supports a wide variety of vintage hardware standards, and runs on inexpensive, compact hardware.
* **Cons:** Requires specific hardware knowledge to set up correctly and is intended for users comfortable with managing disk image files.
