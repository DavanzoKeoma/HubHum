### What it does in plain English
Audiobookshelf acts as your personal media hub dedicated entirely to spoken-word audio, letting you store and stream your collection anywhere. It automatically keeps track of where you left off across all your devices, ensuring you never lose your spot. You can also easily discover, download, and manage your favorite podcasts with automatic episode downloads.

### Who it's for
* Avid audiobook listeners who want to own their library instead of relying on restrictive commercial streaming apps.
* Families or groups of friends sharing a media collection with individual listening progress tracking.
* Podcast enthusiasts looking for an organized, self-managed way to download and listen to episodes.

### Key features
* Fully open-source self-hosted media server with a responsive web interface.
* Multi-user support with custom permissions and individual listening progress synchronization.
* Automatic metadata and cover art fetching from multiple online sources.
* Built-in chapter editor, chapter lookup, and file merging tools for audio files.
* Basic ebook support allowing you to read and send documents directly to your reading devices.

### How to install (Download)
To set up your own server, visit the official website for comprehensive setup instructions, Docker guides, and system requirements: [Official Audiobookshelf Website](https://audiobookshelf.org). You can also test out the live interface via the [Audiobookshelf Demo](https://audiobooks.dev/). For mobile listening, you can find the beta applications on the [Google Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.audiobookshelf.app) and via [TestFlight](https://testflight.apple.com/join/wiic7QIW).

### Pros and Cons
* **Pros:** Completely free and open-source, no recurring subscription fees, active community development, and seamless multi-device sync.
* **Cons:** Requires a computer or home server to run continuously, and setting up reverse proxy routing can be tricky for beginners.
