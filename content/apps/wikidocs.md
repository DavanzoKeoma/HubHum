### What it does in plain English
WikiDocs allows you to create and manage a personal library of notes and documents without relying on complex databases or cloud services. It saves all your information as simple, portable text files directly on your computer. You can keep your work entirely private on your own machine or sync it across devices using your own hosting setup.

### Who it's for
*   **Knowledge Workers:** People who need a reliable, long-term place to store research, project notes, and personal documentation.
*   **Privacy Advocates:** Users who want to avoid "vendor lock-in" and ensure their data remains in a readable, accessible format forever.

### Key features
*   **Total Ownership:** Your data is stored in standard text files, meaning you are never locked into a specific service.
*   **No Database Needed:** The system is lightweight and runs without the complexity of traditional database management.
*   **Flexible Setup:** Use it as a standalone desktop application or host it yourself on a server for remote access.
*   **Modern Editing:** Includes a user-friendly editor that supports images, diagrams, math formulas, and organized page revisions.
*   **Sync Capabilities:** Easily keep your local notes in sync with your self-hosted instance for access from anywhere.

### Pros and Cons
*   **Pros:** Completely free and open-source, keeps data in portable formats, and offers both local and self-hosted options.
*   **Cons:** Self-hosting requires a basic understanding of container software, and the desktop app is best suited for those who prefer managing their own file storage.
